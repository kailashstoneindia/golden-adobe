## 1. Timeout / in-doubt states: how should each be resolved?

### Takeaway

Every Razorpay payment can sit in an "unknown" state for up to 3 days: a payment can show `failed` (timed out) and then move to `authorized` later. Only server-side truth counts, in this order: webhook first, then Fetch Payment/Order APIs, then reconciliation. The client callback is never the source of truth. Order fulfilment must be idempotent, because the webhook and the client-verify call can both trigger it, possibly at the same time.

### Cited Findings

- Razorpay late authorisation: if the bank sends no response, the payment shows `created`. After 10 minutes with no response it is marked `failed` due to timeout. Razorpay then polls the bank at intervals for 3 days from creation. If the bank reports success in that window, the payment is marked `authorized` ("late authorised"). — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- Late authorisation affects "less than 0.5%" of payments on average. Debited but uncaptured funds are auto-refunded. Razorpay recommends subscribing to the `payment.authorized` webhook. — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- Causes named by Razorpay: network issues at the customer's end, technical issues at the customer's bank, and "customers closing the pop-up window or pressing the back button after submitting the OTP". — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- A payment that times out to `failed` "can be Captured or Refunded only if it is late authorised". — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- Razorpay's merchant guide defines a timeout as "an unconfirmed state caused by a missing response", and late authorisation as what happens when that response eventually arrives. (Search snippet only.) — [Razorpay Learn: Payment Timeout vs Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/learn/payment-timeout/)
- Status verification: webhooks are "the primary and most efficient method", delivered asynchronously. For time-sensitive flows where a webhook hasn't arrived, make an immediate Fetch API call for the Payment, Order or Refund. Polling the Payment APIs is also listed. — [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- UPI pending guidance: "If the Order is not marked `paid` within 2-3 minutes, then you can re-initiate payment for the same." Alternatives are to poll the payment `poll` URL until `authorized` or `failed`, poll the Fetch Payments API for the order, or subscribe to payment/order webhooks. — [Razorpay: S2S UPI Collect](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/s2s-integration/payment-methods/upi/collect/)
- One order allows only one successful payment. Many payment attempts can link to one order. Once the order is `paid`, "No further payment requests are allowed". While an order is `attempted` with an `authorised` payment, "initiating another payment using the same order id is not allowed". — [Razorpay: Orders](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/orders/)
- Webhooks are at-least-once and retried with exponential backoff for 24 hours after the event is created. If your server accepts an event but doesn't answer within 5 seconds, the delivery counts as timed out and is resent. After 24 hours of failures the webhook is **disabled** and must be re-enabled manually in the Dashboard. — [Razorpay: Webhook Best Practices](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/best-practices)
- Stripe (for comparison): fulfilment can't rely on the landing page alone, because "a customer can pay successfully and then lose their internet connection before your landing page loads". Webhooks are required. Stripe also recommends triggering fulfilment from the landing page, because "webhooks can sometimes be delayed". The fulfilment function "might be called multiple times, possibly concurrently" and must be safe to call that way. — [Stripe: Fulfil orders](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment)
- Airbnb (Orpheus idempotency framework, 2019): the goal is that if one money-moving operation is called multiple times, money moves at most once. "Connections will drop and timeout at some point." The flow has Pre-RPC, RPC and Post-RPC phases, with idempotency data stored in the primary DB. Errors are classed as retryable or non-retryable. Read repair, write repair and asynchronous repair are used. (Post returned 403; points come from search summaries.) — [Airbnb Engineering](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/avoiding-double-payments-in-a-distributed-payments-system-2981f6b070bb)
- Stripe idempotency: the first response for a key is saved and replayed, including `500` errors. Keys may be pruned after 24 hours. Reusing a key with different parameters is an error. A result is not saved when validation fails or a concurrent request conflicts, so those calls can be retried. GET/DELETE need no key. — [Stripe: Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)
- Shopify: idempotency keys should be unique within the retry window ("typically 24 hours or less"). Shopify prefers ULIDs over UUIDv4 and reported a 50% drop in INSERT time. Shopify reconciles its records against its partners' records, both per transaction and in aggregate. Mismatches are logged as anomalies and auto-fixed where possible. — [Shopify Engineering: Building resilient payment systems](https://shopify.engineering/building-resilient-payment-systems)

## 2. Razorpay payment lifecycle: capture modes, auto-refund windows, late authorization

### Takeaway

States: `created` → `authorized` → `captured` → (`refunded`), or → `failed`. An `authorized` payment that is not captured within **3 days** (current docs) is auto-refunded. Older Razorpay material says **5 days**, so treat 5 days as outdated. Capture settings (auto-capture, auto-capture with a timeout, manual-capture timeout; minimum 12 minutes, maximum 3 days) are the main lever for making late authorizations auto-refund instead of landing on a dead order.

### Cited Findings

- State definitions: `created` means details were sent and the payment is not processed yet. `authorized` means the bank authenticated it and money was deducted from the customer, but it settles only after capture; it is "auto-refunded to the customer if not captured within 3 days of creation". `captured` means verified complete and settled per schedule. `refunded` applies to captured payments. `failed` means an unsuccessful attempt; "Any amount debited will be refunded into customers account in 5-7 working days." — [Razorpay: About Payments](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/)
- Default is auto-capture. Direct Settlement merchants are always auto-captured. "You must ensure that all payments in the authorized state are moved to the captured state within 3 days of creation", otherwise they are refunded automatically. — [Razorpay: Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings)
- Capture options: auto-capture all (anything authorized within 3 days); auto-capture timeout (minimum 12 minutes, maximum/default 3 days); manual capture timeout (minimum 12 minutes, maximum/default 3 days). Auto-refund speed is "Normal Refund" (5–7 working days). — [Razorpay: Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings)
- Capture settings apply only to payments created through the Orders API. Capture values passed in the Orders API override Dashboard settings per payment. — [Razorpay: Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings)
- Example: with auto-capture timeout = 2 days and manual = 3 days, auto-refund happens if not captured by you within 3 days, or if authorized after 3 days. — [Razorpay: Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings)
- Older Razorpay blog: "auto captured as long as authorization happens within **five days**". It gives a capture-timeout example: with a 20-minute capture timeout, "the portal will automatically fail any payment that is authorized later than 20 minutes and will automatically initiate a refund". — [Razorpay Blog: Capture Settings / Late Auth](https://razorpay.com/blog/capture-settings-manage-failed-transactions/). **Conflict:** this contradicts the current docs' 3-day window ([Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings), [About Payments](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/)). Treat 3 days as current.
- A third-party 2026 article also states timeouts of "between 12 minutes and 3 days, with 3 days as the" default. (Search snippet only.) — [OneSol](https://onesol.io/blog/razorpay-payment-failed)
- Order states: `created` lasts until payment is attempted; `attempted` lasts from the first attempt until a payment is captured; `paid` is set after capture and stays `paid` even if refunded. — [Razorpay: Orders](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/orders/)
- "Payments made without an `order_id` cannot be captured and will be automatically refunded." — [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- Stripe comparison: PaymentIntent statuses are `requires_payment_method` → `requires_confirmation` → `requires_action` (3DS) → `processing` (async methods, can take days) / `requires_capture` → `succeeded`, or `canceled`. A failed attempt returns to `requires_payment_method` for retry. — [Stripe: PaymentIntent lifecycle](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/paymentintents/lifecycle)

### Inferences

- **Align the capture timeout with the reservation TTL.** If stock is held for X minutes, set the auto-capture timeout to about X (minimum 12 minutes) per order through the Orders API. Then any authorization that arrives after the hold expires stays uncaptured and is auto-refunded by Razorpay. That turns the "late success after stock release" race (T6) into a no-op on the money side. The trade-off: refunds take 5–7 working days and the customer sees "debited then refunded".
- An alternative is manual capture: capture only after the backend confirms stock is still reserved. Re-check stock, then capture, then commit. This costs one extra API call, and the capture itself can time out (it is idempotent by nature on the same payment, but verify that).
- Our internal state machine should mirror Razorpay's and add `PAYMENT_PENDING` and `EXPIRED_AWAITING_LATE_AUTH` (a 3-day watch window).

### Gaps

- Whether a capture call on an already-captured payment returns an error or success (idempotency of capture) was not verified.
- The exact Razorpay bank-polling schedule inside the 3-day window is not published ("various intervals").

## 3. Method-specific failures (UPI, cards, netbanking) and RBI/NPCI turnaround rules

### Takeaway

RBI's TAT framework (circular RBI/2019-20/67, 20 Sep 2019, effective 15 Oct 2019) requires auto-reversal of failed debits with ₹100/day compensation beyond the TAT. For **UPI payment to merchant** the TAT is **T+5**. For UPI P2P it is T+1. Card-at-merchant (POS) is T+5, and on-us wallets are T+1. UPI is moving to intent/QR: NPCI ended P2P collect on 1 Oct 2025, and Razorpay says merchant UPI Collect is deprecated from 28 Feb 2026 with exemptions. NPCI 2025 rules also throttle how fast PSPs can poll status, so "pending" UPI states last longer.

### Cited Findings

- RBI definition: a failed transaction is one "not fully completed due to any reason not attributable to the customer". T is the calendar date of the transaction. The TAT is "the outer limit". Compensation is credited "without waiting for a complaint or claim". Customers can then escalate to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021. Effective 15 Oct 2019. — [RBI Common Person: Failed Transaction & Compensation Framework](https://www.rbi.org.in/Commonperson/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=3074); circular: [RBI Notification 11693](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693) (direct load redirected to the RBI homepage; content taken from the Common Person page and press coverage)
- UPI rows: (a) fund transfer, debited but beneficiary not credited: beneficiary bank auto-reverses by T+1, ₹100/day beyond T+1. (b) **Payment to merchant**, debited but no confirmation at the merchant: **auto-reversal within T+5**, ₹100/day beyond T+5. — [RBI Common Person page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Commonperson/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=3074)
- Card rows: card-to-card, T+1. POS (card present), debited but no charge slip: auto-reversal T+5, ₹100/day beyond. Card Not Present (e-commerce), debited but no confirmation at the merchant's system: the summariser saw no separate timeline for this row. It is probably a merged cell shared with the POS T+5 row (unverified). — [RBI Common Person page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Commonperson/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=3074)
- IMPS T+1. PPI (wallet) on-us, PPI debited with no merchant confirmation: reversal T+1, ₹100/day. PPI off-us follows the underlying rail's rules (UPI, card network, IMPS). — [RBI Common Person page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Commonperson/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=3074)
- A 2026 press explainer confirms the circular date (20 Sep 2019), the effective date (15 Oct 2019), the T+1/T+5 split, and automatic compensation. — [LiveMint (Oct 2026)](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/failed-upi-atm-or-card-payment-heres-when-youll-get-100-per-day-for-a-delayed-refund-key-rbi-rules-explained-11792316144393.html)
- NPCI API-usage circular (26 Apr 2025, as reported): PSPs must wait **90 seconds** after the original transaction before the first "check transaction status" call, with a maximum of **3** calls within **2 hours**. Response time for Check Status and Reversal was cut from 30s to 10s. Compliance deadline 31 Jul 2025. — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-npci-banks-balance-upi-transaction-guidelines/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-api-update-daily-caps-npci-paytm-phonepe-july-2025-125061600735_1.html); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/upi-status-check-rules-tightened-npci-moves-to-prevent-repeat-outages). **Conflict:** some reports say a later change allows the first check after 45–60 seconds; date unconfirmed (search summary). The NPCI circular itself was not read.
- NPCI circular of 29 Jul 2025 (as reported): from 1 Oct 2025, "UPI P2P Collect shall not be allowed to be processed". Merchant collect requests reportedly stayed operational at that time. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/npci-to-tighten-upi-rules-peer-to-peer-collect-feature-to-end-in-october-489244-2025-08-13); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/08/223-npci-p2p-collect-payments-oct-1-what-it-means/)
- Razorpay: "According to NPCI guidelines, the UPI Collect flow is being deprecated effective **28 February 2026**." Exemptions: MCC 6012/6211, **iOS app and mobile web**, UPI Mandates (execute/modify/revoke), eRupi, and PACB cross-border. Others "must migrate to UPI Intent or UPI QR code". — [Razorpay: S2S UPI Collect](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/s2s-integration/payment-methods/upi/collect/)
- UPI Collect `expiry_time` is in minutes, default 5, maximum 5760. Razorpay advises a custom expiry based on business need and validating the VPA before collect. — [Razorpay: S2S UPI Collect](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/s2s-integration/payment-methods/upi/collect/)
- Card OTP/back-button: closing the pop-up or pressing back after submitting the OTP is a named cause of late authorisation. — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- Checkout behaviour: on failure, "the checkout is displayed again to facilitate payment retry". "Only successful authorisations are auto-submitted" to the handler. — [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- The Checkout `timeout` option (seconds; e.g. 300 = 5 minutes) makes Checkout unusable after expiry. A separate transaction timeout (reportedly 3–15 minutes) applies once a payment is attempted. `modal.ondismiss` fires when the user closes the modal. (Search summary of the Razorpay FAQ.) — [Razorpay: Standard Checkout Troubleshooting & FAQs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/web-integration/standard/troubleshooting-faqs/)

## 4. PSP outages / degraded mode: retries, backoff, circuit breakers, downtime signals

### Takeaway

Retry only idempotent operations (GET/fetch, or creates guarded by our own attempt row). Use capped exponential backoff with jitter, retry at one layer only, and budget retries with a token bucket. Wrap Razorpay calls in a circuit breaker. Use Razorpay's `payment.downtime.*` webhooks and Downtime API to steer customers away from broken methods instead of letting them fail.

### Cited Findings

- Base timeouts on the downstream latency percentile (e.g. p99.9) for a target false-timeout rate. — [AWS Builders' Library: Timeouts, retries and backoff with jitter](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter)
- "Retries are selfish". During overload "retries can make the overload worse". "APIs with side effects aren't safe to retry unless they provide idempotency". — [AWS Builders' Library](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter)
- Exponential backoff with a cap plus a limited number of attempts; "Jitter adds some amount of randomness to the backoff"; "retry at a single point in the stack"; limit retries locally "using a token bucket". — [AWS Builders' Library](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter)
- Shopify: "set low timeouts everywhere you can". A starting point is a 1s open timeout and a 5s read/write timeout; the Ruby `Net::HTTP` default is 60s. A circuit breaker (Semian) fails fast once a service is detected as down. Shopify Payments scopes circuits **per merchant country** so a local outage doesn't spread. Queues grow at about 70–80% utilisation, which calls for rate limiting and load shedding. — [Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-resilient-payment-systems)
- Razorpay downtime webhooks: `payment.downtime.started`, `payment.downtime.updated`, `payment.downtime.resolved`. — [Razorpay: All Webhook Events](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/all)
- The downtime payload includes `method` (e.g. `card`), `begin`, `end`, `status`, `scheduled` (planned or not), `severity` (e.g. `high`) and an `instrument` (e.g. bank/network). (From a search excerpt of the payments webhook page.) — [Razorpay: Payments Webhooks](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/payments)
- Downtime API: `GET /v1/payments/downtimes` (fetch all) and fetch-by-id. Razorpay suggests using it "if you have not received any webhook notifications". Severity values (high/medium/low) and the method list were confirmed only by third-party mirrors. — [Razorpay API: Fetch all downtimes](https://razorpay.com/docs/api/payments/downtime/fetch-all); [Razorpay: Downtime (rainy-day)](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/rainy-day/downtime)

### Inferences — retry-safety matrix for our NestJS service

| Operation                                           | Safe to auto-retry?                | Notes                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fetch payment / order / payments-of-order / refunds | Yes                                | GET is idempotent; backoff plus jitter.                                                                              |
| Create order                                        | Conditionally                      | Guard with our own `checkout_attempt` row and `receipt`. An orphan order is harmless (T1).                           |
| Capture                                             | Probably (same payment_id)         | Fetch first if the result is unknown; capture is not double-counted on one payment, but this is unverified (§2 gap). |
| Refund                                              | **Only after fetch-refunds check** | A blind retry risks a double refund (T8).                                                                            |

Per method, use downtime webhooks to hide or warn on affected UPI/bank/card networks. Scope breakers per method or bank, as Shopify does per country.
- The NPCI status-check throttle (§3) means our own UPI status polling should back off to minutes, not seconds.

## 5. Our own infra failures and races (DB, crashes, deploys, queues, clock skew, sweeper)

### Takeaway

Never wrap an external PSP call inside a DB transaction. Persist intent before the call and the outcome after it, with a repair path for "call made, outcome not saved". Every state change must be a guarded, idempotent compare-and-set, so webhook, client-verify, reconciler and stale-reservation sweeper can race safely. Webhook intake must be fast-ack plus durable queue plus dedupe.

### Cited Findings

- Airbnb Orpheus splits work into Pre-RPC (record intent in the DB), RPC (the network call, outside the transaction) and Post-RPC (record the result). Idempotency data lives in the primary DB for strong consistency. Read/write/async repair is used. (Search summary; post returned 403.) — [Airbnb Engineering](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/avoiding-double-payments-in-a-distributed-payments-system-2981f6b070bb)
- Razorpay webhooks: at-least-once; a server that "accepts the event but fails to respond in 5 seconds" will receive it again. Dedupe by the `x-razorpay-event-id` header (unique per event). — [Razorpay: Webhook Best Practices](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/best-practices); [Razorpay: Validate & Test](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/validate-test)
- Order is not guaranteed: `payment.authorized` then `payment.captured` "may not be followed at all times. You should configure your webhook URL to not expect delivery of these events in this order." — [Razorpay: Validate & Test](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/validate-test)
- After 24h of continuous failure the webhook is disabled, with an alert email to the configured address. — [Razorpay: Webhook Best Practices](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/best-practices)
- Stripe: the fulfilment function may be "called multiple times, possibly concurrently" and must handle that. — [Stripe: Fulfil orders](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment)
- Stripe idempotency doesn't save the result when "the request conflicts with another request that's executing concurrently". This is a concurrency guard pattern worth copying in our own idempotency layer. — [Stripe: Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)

### Inferences — infra failure catalogue

| #   | Scenario                                                                                            | Observed                                                                 | Handling                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | If mishandled                                                     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I1  | DB commit fails after PSP success (e.g. verify call got a valid signature, then the DB write threw) | Razorpay says paid; our DB says pending                                  | The webhook and reconciler re-drive the same idempotent `confirmOrder`. ## 6. Inventory interplay: reserve-then-pay vs pay-then-reserve, TTL alignment, oversell after late success

### Takeaway

Reserve-then-pay with a TTL, aligned to Razorpay's capture timeout, is the safer default for physical stock. The money-side tool to make late success harmless is the per-order capture timeout (minimum 12 minutes), so late authorizations auto-refund instead of becoming captured money on an expired order.

### Cited Findings

- The auto-capture timeout is configurable per order through the Orders API (overriding the Dashboard), minimum 12 minutes, maximum 3 days. Payments authorized after the timeout are not auto-captured and are auto-refunded if not captured manually within the manual window. — [Razorpay: Capture Settings](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/capture-settings)
- Razorpay's own example: a 20-minute capture timeout "will automatically fail any payment that is authorized later than 20 minutes and will automatically initiate a refund". — [Razorpay Blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/capture-settings-manage-failed-transactions/)
- Late authorisation can arrive up to 3 days after creation. — [Razorpay: Late Authorisation](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payments/late-authorisation)
- Stripe lists "Update inventory or stock records" as part of the idempotent fulfilment function, not the client flow. — [Stripe: Fulfil orders](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment)

### Inferences

| Strategy                                                                               | Pros                                                                                      | Cons / failure mode                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Reserve-then-pay** (hold stock at checkout start, TTL X)                             | No oversell while the hold lives; clear UX                                                | Holds expire before late auth (up to 3d), so you must decide revive vs refund (T6). Abandoned carts lock stock.                     |
| **Pay-then-reserve** (decrement at payment confirmation)                               | No stuck holds                                                                            | Oversell on popular/limited items: two payers, one unit, so a refund after the customer already paid (bad UX, refund TAT 5–7 days). |
| **Hybrid**: reserve + auto-capture timeout ≈ reservation TTL + manual-capture fallback | Late auth beyond the TTL stays uncaptured and auto-refunds; within the TTL it is captured | Needs the timeout set per order (minimum 12 minutes); UPI pending needs a TTL ≥ about 12–15 minutes.                                |

- TTL ordering rule (inference): `checkout timeout option` < `reservation TTL` ≈ `auto-capture timeout` < `sweeper grace` (reservation TTL plus a buffer covering Razorpay's 10-minute bank-timeout marking). For a single-unit artisan/vendor listing (as in this codebase), revive-if-stock-free, else refund, is the T6 policy.
- With auto-capture-all (the default, 3 days), any late auth will be **captured** even on an expired order. You then must issue an API refund yourself, or you're holding customer money for goods you won't ship.

### Gaps

- No primary engineering blog (Flipkart/Swiggy/Zomato) on reservation TTL vs payment TTL was found in this pass. The guidance above is inferred from Razorpay mechanics.

## 7. Fraud/abuse failure modes: disputes, tampered amounts, signature forgery

### Takeaway

Never trust the client for amount, success or order_id. Create the order server-side with the amount computed from the DB. Verify `HMAC_SHA256(our_stored_order_id + "|" + payment_id, key_secret)`, and verify webhooks with the webhook secret over the **raw body**. Confirm the payment's amount and order_id from a Fetch call or the webhook payload. Subscribe to `payment.dispute.*` webhooks and act on `respond_by` deadlines.

### Cited Findings

- Signature: an HMAC SHA256 hex digest of `order_id|razorpay_payment_id` using the key secret; it must match `razorpay_signature`. "Do not use the `razorpay_order_id` returned by Checkout"; use the order_id from your server. — [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- React Native guidance (search summary): signature verification on the server is mandatory and must never happen in the app. "Never fulfil an order based solely on the client-side result." Create an order server-side for every payment. — [Razorpay: React Native (Quickbuy embedded)](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/features/quickbuy/embedded/react-native); [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- Amounts are in the currency subunit (paise); e.g. `29935` = ₹299.35. — [Razorpay: Integration Steps](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-gateway/quick-integration/integration-steps/)
- Webhook signature: `X-Razorpay-Signature` is an HMAC over the payload with the webhook secret. "ensure that the webhook body passed as an argument is the raw webhook request body. Do not parse or cast the webhook request body." — [Razorpay: Validate & Test](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/validate-test)
- Whitelist Razorpay webhook IPs; TLS 1.2+ required. — [Razorpay: Webhook Best Practices](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/best-practices)
- Dispute webhooks: `payment.dispute.created` (raised by the issuing bank), `.won`, `.lost`, `.closed`, `.under_review` (evidence submitted), `.action_required` (evidence insufficient/unreadable/amount mismatch). Sample payloads include `respond_by` (Unix timestamp), `phase` (`chargeback` or `fraud`) and `amount_deducted` (non-zero in the `lost` sample). — [Razorpay: Dispute Webhooks](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/disputes)
- Refund webhooks: `refund.created`, `refund.processed`, `refund.failed`, `refund.speed_changed`. — [Razorpay: All Webhook Events](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/all)

### Inferences

| #   | Scenario                                                        | Handling                                                                                                                                                             | If mishandled                                                  |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| F1  | Client tampers with the amount (modified RN bundle / proxy)     | The server computes the amount from the DB cart and creates the order; on confirm, assert `payment.amount == order.amount` and `payment.order_id == stored order_id` | Goods sold at an attacker-chosen price.                        |
| F2  | Forged success callback (fake payment_id/signature)             | Server HMAC check with the stored order_id, plus a Fetch Payment status check (`captured`/`authorized`)                                                              | Free goods.                                                    |
| F3  | Replayed valid signature from an earlier order onto a new order | The signature binds to the order_id; use our stored order_id; unique `payment_id` constraint                                                                         | Replays one payment across orders.                             |
| F4  | Forged webhook                                                  | Verify `X-Razorpay-Signature` on the raw body (NestJS: enable `rawBody` and don't use the parsed JSON for HMAC); optional IP allowlist                               | Fake "paid" events.                                            |
| F5  | Chargeback/dispute                                              | On `payment.dispute.created`, flag the order, freeze payout to the vendor, collect evidence before `respond_by`; on `.lost`, record the deduction                    | Missed deadlines mean automatic loss; vendor already paid out. |
| F6  | Friendly fraud: "debited but failed" claims                     | Show the Razorpay payment status and refund ARN; RBI TAT messaging                                                                                                   | Double refunds.                                                |

### Gaps

- The Razorpay dispute deadline policy (how `respond_by` is set, and the consequences of missing it) is not documented on the page read.
- I didn't verify whether Razorpay offers a timestamp-based replay protection on webhook signatures.
